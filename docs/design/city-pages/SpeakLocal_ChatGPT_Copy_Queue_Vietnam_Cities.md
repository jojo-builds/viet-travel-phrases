### Ba Na Hills (Bà Nà Hills) — Đà Nẵng — Theme Park / Landmark

#### Why go

Perched 1,500 m above Đà Nẵng in the Trường Sơn range, Sun World Ba Na Hills combines a mountain resort with a sprawling theme park. French colonists built the original hill station in 1919 to escape the coastal heat, and today the resort's centerpiece is the Golden Bridge: a 148.6 m pedestrian bridge held aloft by gigantic stone hands at an altitude of 1,414 m. The complex also houses a medieval-style French Village with cobbled squares, a Gothic-inspired church and vintage cars serenaded by French pop music, along with nine themed gardens and a 100 m Debay Wine Cellar built in 1923 that stays at 16-20 degrees C year-round. Reaching the mountaintop by cable car, including the 5,801 m non-stop line, is an experience in itself.

#### Mist-cooled alpine town in the clouds

The ascent begins with a 15-20 minute cable-car ride over jungle valleys. As the cabins climb, the temperature drops several degrees compared with Đà Nẵng and mist swirls around the treetops. At the summit you emerge into crisp mountain air scented with pine and flowers. The Golden Bridge feels otherworldly: its balustrades are lined with chrysanthemums and the stone hands appear to burst through the hillside. Wander through the French Village's stone courtyards, where accordion music floats past faux-medieval facades. Nearby, the Linh Ứng area offers a quieter moment: a 27 m seated Buddha rising from a lotus base and gardens of hydrangeas and orchids. Fantasy Park, Vietnam's largest indoor arcade, buzzes with 3D/4D rides and Jules Verne-inspired games.

#### What to do or try

- **Golden Bridge & cable car** – Walk the entire 148.6 m arc of the Golden Bridge for photo-ready views over misty valleys. Ride the Ba Na cable car for the long glide above forested slopes.
- **Explore the French Village** – Sip a cappuccino in the town square, visit the Gothic-style church and wander past vintage Citroën cars while French chansons play.
- **Jardin d'Amour & Debay Wine Cellar** – Stroll through nine differently themed gardens and duck into the Debay Wine Cellar, a 100 m tunnel carved into the mountain.
- **Alpine Coaster & Fantasy Park** – Try the Alpine Coaster or play classic arcade games and watch 4D films in Fantasy Park.

#### Best moment

Visit during the dry season from February to August when skies are clearer and rain is less likely. To beat the crowds and enjoy soft morning light on the Golden Bridge, arrive before 8 a.m. Late afternoon is also quieter, and sunset from the bridge or the summit can be spectacular.

#### Nearby context

Ba Na Hills is about 42 km west of Đà Nẵng city. From the summit you can see the East Sea on clear days and, on descending, detour to nearby attractions such as the Marble Mountains or Mỹ Sơn Sanctuary. Plan at least half a day here.

#### Useful phrases

- **“Mua vé cáp treo cho hai người.”** – Two cable-car tickets, please.
- **“Cầu Vàng ở đâu?”** – Where is the Golden Bridge?
- **“Vườn nho Đebay mở cửa mấy giờ?”** – What time does the Debay wine cellar open?

### Museum of Cham Sculpture — Đà Nẵng — Museum

#### Why go

Occupying a 6,673 m² site along Đà Nẵng's riverfront, this museum holds the world's largest collection of artifacts from the Champa civilization. French archaeologists commissioned the original building in 1915 and opened it in 1919. Today it displays more than 2,000 sculptures dating from the 5th-15th centuries, with about 500 on view in airy halls. Nine pieces are registered as national treasures, including the Mỹ Sơn E1 altar depicting Vishnu reclining on the serpent Shesha, the Trà Kiệu altar covered with apsara dancers, a Tara Bodhisattva statue and the Đồng Dương pedestal.

#### Walking through history

The museum's low-slung, ochre-colored building combines colonial and Cham architectural motifs. Inside, sunlight filters onto sandstone statues of Hindu gods Shiva, Vishnu and Brahma, mythical creatures like Makara and Nāga, and fertility symbols such as lingam-yoni sets. In the E Gallery you will find the famed Mỹ Sơn E1 altar depicting Vishnu on a multi-headed serpent. Nearby, the Trà Kiệu altar shows celestial dancers with flowing robes carved in high relief, while the Tara Bodhisattva gazes serenely from the Đồng Dương pedestal. Placards explain the symbolism and context, and the quiet galleries offer respite from Đà Nẵng's busy streets.

#### What to do or try

- **National treasures trail** – Start in the E Gallery to see the Mỹ Sơn E1 altar, then move through to the Trà Kiệu altar and Tara Bodhisattva. Notice how Hindu and Buddhist motifs blend.
- **Architectural tour** – Appreciate the building itself, especially its arches, terracotta tiles and Cham temple references.
- **Educational exhibits** – Use bilingual labels and audio guides to understand Champa history and the significance of each artifact.
- **Museum shop** – Browse for books on Cham art and small sculpture replicas.

#### Best moment

The museum opens at 7:30 a.m. and closes at 5:00 p.m. Arrive soon after opening to explore the galleries in relative quiet before tour groups arrive.

#### Nearby context

After visiting, stroll 1.5 km to the Love Bridge, where heart-shaped lampposts overlook the Hàn River. Đà Nẵng's downtown area is nearby, with colonial-era buildings and modern cafes. For a snack, detour to Cồn Market, home to thousands of stalls and dishes like grilled pork vermicelli and bánh xèo.

#### Useful phrases

- **“Cho tôi hai vé vào bảo tàng.”** – Two tickets for the museum, please.
- **“Bàn thờ Mỹ Sơn E1 ở phòng nào?”** – Which room is the Mỹ Sơn E1 altar in?
- **“Có hướng dẫn viên tiếng Anh không?”** – Do you have English-language guides?

### Perfume River (Sông Hương) — Huế — River / Cultural Route

#### Why go

Flowing some 80 km from the Trường Sơn mountains to Tam Giang Lagoon, the Perfume River is the spine of Huế. It earns its name from the aromatic plants and blossoms that fall into the water upstream. The Nguyễn emperors sited their imperial capital beside this gentle river; it glides past iconic landmarks such as Thiên Mụ Pagoda and the tombs of Minh Mạng and Khải Định. A boat trip here is central to understanding Huế's slower, more poetic rhythm.

#### On the river

Board a dragon-prowed boat and drift along calm water as the city unfolds: fishermen casting nets, floating houses and vendors selling snacks from sampans. Pass Dã Viên Bridge and the slender tower of Thiên Mụ Pagoda before the river bends past royal tombs. At night the water glistens under moonlight and the songs of boat rowers break the silence, creating a scene celebrated in poetry and folk songs. Locals also gather on the banks for festivals and performances.

#### What to do or try

- **Sunset or evening cruise** – Hire a dragon boat at the river quay for a one- or two-hour cruise past Thiên Mụ Pagoda and the citadel. Evening trips let you see the river shimmering under lantern light and hear traditional folk songs.
- **Tea cruise** – Some operators offer afternoon tea cruises with herbal tea, cakes and fruit, along with commentary on Huế's history and river legends.
- **Riverside strolls** – Walk along the embankments to catch glimpses of everyday life: fishermen mending nets, children swimming and monks moving between pagodas.
- **Visit river temples** – Combine your boat trip with stops at Hòn Chén Temple or climb the steps to Thiên Mụ Pagoda for panoramic views.

#### Best moment

Choose a late-afternoon cruise to enjoy the changing light and cooler temperatures. As dusk falls, the river sparkles and the songs of boat rowers echo across the water. Morning cruises are quieter, and the riverbanks feel especially soft before the day heats up.

#### Nearby context

The Perfume River runs through Huế's historic center. On its northern bank lies the Imperial City; to the south are colonial villas and tree-lined boulevards. Combine a river cruise with visits to Thiên Mụ Pagoda, Hòn Chén Temple or the royal tombs scattered upstream. Dragon boats also run to Thủy Biều village, where you can sample pomelo candy and stroll through fruit orchards.

#### Useful phrases

- **“Thuê thuyền rồng một giờ bao nhiêu?”** – How much is a one-hour dragon-boat ride?
- **“Chúng tôi muốn đi đến chùa Thiên Mụ.”** – We'd like to go to Thiên Mụ Pagoda.
- **“Cảm ơn vì bài hát!”** – Thank you for the song.

### Bà Lễ Well (Bale Well) — Hội An — Restaurant

#### Why go

Hidden down an alley between Trần Hưng Đạo and Phan Châu Trinh streets, Giếng Bà Lễ is one of Hội An's worst-kept secrets. There is no menu: you order the set meal and the staff bring platters of barbecued pork, crispy bánh xèo pancakes, fried spring rolls, fresh herbs and rice-paper wrappers. The meal is cheap and plentiful, and the friendly women working here show you how to assemble your own rolls. The restaurant takes its name from an ancient well dating to the 10th-century Champa era, said to be tied to the water used for cao lầu noodles.

#### Smell the grill before you find it

Finding Bale Well is part of the fun. You will smell smoky pork on the charcoal grill before you see the open-air dining area tucked down a narrow lane. Customers sit at metal tables that spill into the alley, and there is a convivial, unpolished atmosphere. Wrapping your own rolls can be messy; juices drip down your fingers and herbs crunch in every bite. After you have eaten your fill, the staff might surprise you with a small chocolate or mango mousse dessert.

#### What to order or try

- **The set meal** – Expect skewers of smoky pork, bánh xèo pancakes, fried spring rolls, rice-paper wrappers, a basket of fresh herbs and a sweet-spicy dipping sauce.
- **Hands-on wrapping** – Staff will demonstrate how to pile pork, pancake and herbs onto the rice paper, roll it tight and dip it.
- **Add drinks** – Order a cold beer or iced tea; drinks are extra but inexpensive.
- **Dessert surprise** – Do not be surprised when a small mousse arrives at the end of your meal.

#### Best moment

Bale Well opens from 9:30 a.m. to 9:30 p.m. Arrive early for lunch to avoid waiting for a table; evenings tend to be busier as locals and backpackers cram the alley. The grill operates all day, so there is no bad time to drop in for a hands-on meal.

#### Nearby context

The restaurant sits a few minutes' walk north of Hội An's Japanese Covered Bridge and the lantern-lit streets of the Old Town. After eating, wander through the nearby night market or visit historic houses such as Phùng Hưng. If you are lost, follow the smell of smoke and the sound of conversation down the alley.

#### Useful phrases

- **“Một suất ăn, cảm ơn.”** – One set meal, please.
- **“Làm sao để cuốn bánh tráng?”** – How do I wrap the rice paper?
- **“Cho thêm rau và nước chấm nhé?”** – Could we have more herbs and dipping sauce?

### Bùi Viện Walking Street — Ho Chi Minh City — Nightlife Street

#### Why go

Bùi Viện, in the heart of the Phạm Ngũ Lão area, has long been Ho Chi Minh City's backpacker district. In 2017 the city converted it into a pedestrian-only zone on weekend evenings, modeling it on Bangkok's Khao San Road. From Friday to Sunday the street closes to traffic from 7 p.m. to 2 a.m., and it transforms into a dense mosaic of bars, hostels, souvenir stalls and street performers. Neon lights flash, music booms from every corner and the aroma of grilling seafood and bánh mì fills the air. It is one of Asia's most recognizable nightlife strips and a magnet for both international visitors and young Vietnamese.

#### A sensory overload after dark

By day, Bùi Viện is a tangle of travel agencies, massage parlors and cafes; at night it becomes Saigon's loudest street. Fire dancers, DJs, break-dancers and street magicians perform in the pedestrian zone on weekend nights. Bars line the pavement, offering two-for-one cocktails and beer buckets; travelers perch on tiny plastic stools while crowds spill into the road. Food stalls sell sizzling seafood, bánh mì and spicy noodles until late. Police patrols maintain order, but keep belongings close as petty theft can occur in dense crowds.

#### What to do or try

- **Bar-hop** – Start early to catch happy-hour deals before 9 p.m. Sample craft beers, cocktails or Saigon-style bia hơi while soaking up the atmosphere.
- **Street-food crawl** – Try grilled squid, skewers or a bánh mì from vendors operating well into the night.
- **Catch live acts** – Look out for fire shows, live bands and impromptu karaoke in the pedestrian zone. Many bars feature rock, reggae or Vietnamese pop.
- **People-watch** – Pull up a stool and watch backpackers, locals and buskers mingle. Bargaining is common at souvenir stalls but not in bars.

#### Best moment

The street truly comes alive between 7 p.m. and 2 a.m. on Friday, Saturday and Sunday when it becomes vehicle-free. Arrive just after sundown to see neon lights flicker on and street vendors set up. Weeknights are quieter but still lively; avoid bringing valuables and keep an eye on your belongings.

#### Nearby context

Bùi Viện is part of a triangle with Đề Thám and Phạm Ngũ Lão streets. It lies a short walk from Ben Thanh Market, the Fine Arts Museum and Nguyễn Huệ Walking Street. During the day explore the War Remnants Museum or grab a bánh mì at Bánh Mì Huỳnh Hoa before returning to Bùi Viện for its nighttime metamorphosis.

#### Useful phrases

- **“Đêm nay phố đi bộ mở mấy giờ?”** – What time does the walking street open tonight?
- **“Một chai bia nữa, cảm ơn.”** – Another beer, please.
- **“Túi này có an toàn không?”** – Is this area safe for my bag?
