# Gate 1 Pass 1: Traveler Utility Review

Approval: APPROVE

- Splitting the current broad `practical-service-navigation` lane into ride, directions, hotel, money, and food classes is the right structural move; it should make Tagalog feel materially more useful, not just larger.
- The promoted hubs are concentrated in real traveler-friction moments: pickup/route correction, booking-name/check-in, key card/hot water/Wi-Fi, total/change/write-total/card failure, and menu/allergy/to-go are exactly the kinds of breakdowns people need fast.
- Keeping all existing `24` hubs and deepening them with class-specific module mixes should improve navigability and avoid the generic textbook feel that a single shared module pattern would risk.
- No blocker, but if anything needs trimming, cut secondary convenience hubs before core friction hubs: late checkout, best seller, no ice, and avoid-the-toll/ATM-first are easier to defer than arrival, directions, payment clarity, and hotel problem-solving.
